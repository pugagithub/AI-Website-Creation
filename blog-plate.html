A simple way to build
a balanced plate

A no-counting framework for meals that feel satisfying and support your goals.

02build
your
plate

Nutrition advice can get complicated quickly. There are numbers to track, rules to remember, and a long list of foods you are told to avoid.

A balanced plate brings the focus back to something useful: making a meal that gives you energy and leaves you satisfied. You do not need perfect portions or a special ingredient list to begin.

The three-part plate

Start by filling about half your plate with vegetables or fruit. Add a palm-sized portion of protein — eggs, lentils, yoghurt, fish, chicken, tofu, or beans. Finish with a portion of carbohydrates that you enjoy, such as rice, potatoes, oats, or roti.

Add a little fat for flavour and satisfaction: nuts, seeds, olive oil, or avocado. The goal is not to make every plate identical. It is to create a helpful rhythm you can use anywhere.

A good meal should support your goals and still feel like something you want to eat.

Use the framework, not the rules

Some days will look different. That is normal. If lunch is lighter on protein, you can make dinner more substantial. If you are eating out, look for one protein, one vegetable, and a carbohydrate you enjoy. Flexible structure beats rigid perfection.

Keep it simple,
